How it works, and what to keep in mind

Method

Text is lowercased, negations such as "not" and "never" are kept, common filler words are removed and words are lightly stemmed. Single words and neighbouring word pairs become TF-IDF features, so "not good" and "good" look different to the network.

The classifier is a feed-forward deep neural network with up to two ReLU hidden layers, dropout, L2 weight decay and a 3-way softmax output, trained with mini-batch Adam. The code is written from scratch in JavaScript, with no libraries, which is why it runs entirely in your browser.

Word influence uses occlusion: each word is removed in turn and the change in the positive-minus-negative score is measured. Aspect sentiment scores each clause that mentions an aspect keyword separately.

Limits

ds1, ds2 and ds3 are generated from hand-written phrase banks in the style of real feedback, with mixed reviews and about 4% label noise added. They are suitable for demonstrating and comparing the framework, but accuracy on them will not match real-world data.

For production, replace the data with real corpora such as Amazon product reviews, Yelp or airline tweets, and move to embeddings, LSTM or transformer encoders for better handling of sarcasm and long text.

Use ds4 to test the framework on your own labeled comments.
